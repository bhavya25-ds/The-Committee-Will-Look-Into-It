## Chart 1 

### Delhi Districts by Section 144 Order Count

**Observations:**
- The highest Sec 144 counts are in Shahdara (578), North-East (541), North (520), and East (508) districts — not Central Delhi, which sits near the bottom at just 28 orders. 
-This is the opposite of what anyone would expect if Sec 144 was primarily being used to suppress protest near Parliament and Jantar Mantar.
- **Shahdara** and **North-East Delhi** are predominantly `working-class`, `Muslim-majority areas`. These districts saw severe communal violence during the Delhi riots of February 2020, and the anti-CAA protests that preceded them were heavily concentrated here. 
- Sec 144 being disproportionately imposed in these areas — while Central Delhi (Parliament, Connaught Place) sits at 28 — suggests the tool is being used **less** as `protest suppression at the political center` and **more** as `population control in specific communities`. 
- Central District's low count is also explained by the fact that blanket Sec 144 there is typically imposed through emergency orders that may not appear in this dataset's count methodology.
- The Metro, IGI, Railway, and PHQ entries at the bottom (112, 6, 5, 2) are operational units, not geographic districts — their low counts reflect that Sec 144 is applied to specific choke points rather than transit infrastructure broadly.

## Chart 2

### Top States by Internet Shutdowns (2024–2026)

**Observations:**
- J&K leads at 20, Manipur at 14, Odisha and Rajasthan tied at 12. This is your entire dataset's cumulative picture across 2024–2026.
- J&K at the top is a structural reality — the state has been under repeated internet restrictions since 2019 and the abrogation of Article 370. 
- Even within 2024–2026, the pattern hasn't broken. 20 shutdowns in roughly two and a half years means **J&K** averages about **8 shutdowns per year**, continuing what has been called the `longest running internet restriction regime in any democracy`.
- Manipur at **14** reflects the tail end of the `2023 ethnic violence between Meitei and Kuki-Zo communities`. The first shutdown started May 3, 2023, lasted **over 200 days**, and by 2024 the state was still recording repeated short-duration orders triggered by ongoing violence. 
- Odisha at 12 is the surprise. Odisha doesn't have the same profile as J&K or Manipur but matching Rajasthan at 12 shutdowns over this period is significant. Odisha's shutdowns in this period are primarily `exam-related` and `communal tension-triggered`.
- Delhi appearing at 4 in this chart is directly tied to the `CJP protest` activity in 2026, including the `July 20 'Chalo Sansad' shutdown` where mobile internet was cut in parts of Central Delhi with **no published order**.

## Chart 3

### Preventive vs Reactive Shutdowns (63 vs 36)

**Observations:**
- **63** preventive, **36 reactive** — 64% of all shutdowns in the dataset are preventive.
- A **reactive shutdown** means something already happened — violence, a crisis, a riot — and the government cut communication to contain the fallout. 
- A **preventive** shutdown means the government cut communication because it anticipated protest or unrest. **No triggering event. No published order in many cases**. Just **pre-emptive silencing**.
- In 2026 this played out explicitly: on July 20, the CJP organized the 'Chalo Sansad' march from Jantar Mantar to Parliament demanding the Education Minister's resignation over the NEET 2026 paper leak. Mobile internet was suspended in parts of Central Delhi. The Internet Freedom Foundation reported that **no suspension order was published by the Ministry of Home Affairs, the DoT, Delhi government, or Delhi Police — a direct violation of the Anuradha Bhasin Supreme Court ruling and the 2024 Telecom Suspension Rules**. That shutdown is in preventive column. The government cut internet to a peaceful march before anything happened.
- 63 preventive shutdowns across 2024–2026 means this isn't exceptional use of an emergency tool. It's **routine governance**.

## Chart 4 

### Manipur Deep Dive (Nov 2024 – Apr 2026)

**Observations:**
- The chart shows two clear clusters: **Nov–Dec 2024 (4 and 2 shutdowns)** dropping to near-zero through most of 2025, then spiking to **5** in **April 2026**.
- The Nov 2024 cluster is the tail of the **September 2024 wave** — Manipur imposed a **shutdown** on *September 10, 2024* after a curfew was imposed following `fresh ethnic violence and clashes between protesters and police`. Schools shut for over a week. The December drop-off reflects a brief stabilization period.
- The **April 2026 spike** of 5 is the **most recent and the sharpest** on this chart. On April 7, 2026, the Manipur Home Department ordered a three-day suspension across five districts — **Imphal West, Imphal East, Thoubal, Kakching, and Bishnupur** — after a **bomb attack** killed t**wo children**. The same five districts that have been *repeatedly shut down* `since 2023`. The pattern is *identical* to the `original 2023 order`: short-duration framing that historically extended indefinitely.
- The near-zero middle period (Jan–Mar 2025, Aug–Dec 2025) doesn't mean the conflict resolved. It means the shutdown orders either weren't issued formally or weren't captured in the dataset for that window. The violence in Manipur between the Meitei and Kuki-Zo communities has been ongoing since May 2023 with **258+ recorded deaths** as of **November 2024 (2 years ago)**.

## Chart 5

### Year-wise State Breakdown (2024 / 2025 / 2026)

**Observations:**

**2024**: 
- **Manipur** dominates at 6, everything else at 1–2. This is a Manipur-centric year — the ongoing ethnic violence is the sole story driving shutdown volume nationally.

**2025**: 
- Dramatic shift. **J&K** explodes to *16*, **Odisha** jumps to *10*, **Rajasthan** to *7*. **Manipur** falls to just *1*. 
- This is the most geographically distributed year in the data — shutdowns spread across 13 states including Punjab, Bihar, Maharashtra, and Ladakh. 
- J&K's 16 in a single year is the continuation of the structural restriction pattern. Odisha at 10 in 2025 is significant.

**2026**: 
- Manipur jumps back to 7 (April bombing cluster), Rajasthan, J&K, and Delhi each at 4. 
- **Delhi's** *4 shutdowns in 2026* — remembering Delhi had **zero** in **2024** and **zero** in **2025**. This is entirely a **2026 phenomenon** driven by the **NEET controversy and CJP protest activity**. That's the direct data footprint of the movement.

`The year-wise shifts from Manipur-dominated (2024) → nationally distributed (2025) → Manipur + protest-driven Delhi spike (2026)`.

## Chart 6

### Annotated Political Timeline (2024–2026)

**Observations:**
- **2024**: 17 shutdowns. Lowest year in the dataset. NEET 2024 controversy broke in May–June 2024 with widespread allegations of paper leaks and grace marks manipulation for 1.5 million students. Political protests followed but the government contained the narrative — no significant internet shutdowns tied directly to NEET 2024 protest activity appear in the data. The 17 count is almost entirely Manipur.
- **2025**: 54 shutdowns — the peak. This is a 3× jump from 2024. The Manipur violence is still running in the background, but the 2025 spike is driven by J&K (16) and Odisha (10) — neither of which have a single clean political explanation. This is diffuse suppression: many states, many triggers, no single event. That's actually more concerning than a single spike, because it means the shutdown mechanism is being deployed across the country as a default response to any local tension.
- **2026**: 28 shutdowns. Down from 2025 but nearly double 2024. Critically, 2026 is only partially complete in your dataset — the data likely runs to mid-2026 at most, meaning 28 is an undercount. The NEET 2026 exam was held May 3 for 2.27 million aspirants and cancelled May 12 after revelations of paper leaks and NTA insider arrests. The CJP protest movement formally began June 28. The 'Chalo Sansad' march was July 20, with the Delhi internet shutdown following. The 2026 28 count includes that Delhi cluster. If your data captures through July 2026, it's likely the CJP-linked shutdowns are already in there.

## Note for year 2026

- The shape of the chart — low, spike, fall — looks like suppression peaked in 2025 and is coming down. It isn't. 2026 is a partial year and already at 28. Annualized, 2026 would exceed 2024 significantly, and if protest activity continues through the rest of the year, it could approach 2025 levels.
